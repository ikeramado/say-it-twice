## **Your rights under US law**

**You are generally not entitled to automatic cash compensation under US federal law for this delay.** Even though your Delta flight arrived 232 minutes (3 hours and 52 minutes) late, US law does not impose a general requirement on airlines to compensate passengers financially for lengthy delays when they ultimately complete their journey.

There is, however, an important distinction between compensation, refunds, and other assistance.

### **1. Refund of your ticket**

Under the Department of Transportation's (DOT) significant-delay rules, a domestic flight arriving **3 hours or more after its scheduled arrival time** qualifies as a significantly delayed flight.

Your delay was 3 hours and 52 minutes, so it exceeds that threshold.

However, the refund entitlement depends on whether you travel:

- If you had rejected the delayed flight and chosen not to travel, you would have been entitled to a full refund, including applicable taxes and ancillary fees.

- Because you completed the journey, you are **not entitled to a ticket refund under the DOT rules**.


This applies even if your ticket was non-refundable.

### **2. Meals and accommodation**

Delta has made specific commitments to passengers affected by delays within its control.

|Benefit|Your potential entitlement|
|---|---|
|Meal or meal voucher|Yes, if the delay caused you to wait at least 3 hours beyond scheduled departure|
|Hotel accommodation|Yes, if the controllable delay inconvenienced you overnight away from home or your destination|

|Rebooking|Next available flight at no additional cost, where applicable|

These are Delta's published customer-service commitments, not automatic cash compensation under federal law.

Importantly, the 3-hour meal threshold is measured from scheduled departure, not scheduled arrival. Your 232-minute arrival delay alone does not establish that you met this condition.

### **3. Cash compensation for the 3-hour-52-minute delay**

No automatic compensation

Unlike EU Regulation 261/2004, US federal aviation law does not establish a fixed compensation payment based on the length of a domestic flight delay.

Consequently, you cannot automatically claim:

- $250, $400, or $600 based on delay duration.

- A percentage of your ticket price.

- A fixed payment for inconvenience or lost time.


Delta can nevertheless voluntarily offer compensation, including:

- Cash equivalents or gift cards.

- Travel credits or vouchers.

- SkyMiles.


Delta expressly reserves discretion to provide these benefits depending on the individual circumstances.

### **4. Does the cause of the delay matter?**

Yes. It matters particularly for Delta's customer-service commitments.

Examples of controllable causes include:

- Mechanical or maintenance problems.

- Crew scheduling problems.

- Aircraft cleaning.

- Baggage loading and fueling.


Weather and air traffic control restrictions are generally outside Delta's control.

The DOT can hold airlines accountable for failing to honor their published commitments for controllable delays.
