Under U.S. federal law, you are **not entitled to any monetary compensation or a ticket refund** because you took the flight and reached your destination.

Unlike European regulations (EU261/UK261), U.S. Department of Transportation (DOT) rules do not require airlines to pay cash compensation for delayed flights.

### How U.S. Law & Airline Policies Apply to Your Flight

- **Ticket Refunds:** Under the DOT’s automatic refund rule, a domestic delay of **3 hours or more** (180+ minutes) qualifies as a "significant delay". However, that rule only entitles you to a cash refund **if you choose not to travel** on the delayed flight and reject alternative rebooking. Once you board and complete the flight, your right to a ticket refund is forfeited.

- **Cash Compensation:** There is no federal law requiring U.S. airlines to pay out cash or vouchers for inconvenience or lost time due to delays. (A 2024 DOT proposal that would have mandated cash payouts for severe airline-caused delays was formally withdrawn in 2025).

    Travel Tourister+ 1

- **Ancillary Service Refunds:** The only statutory refund you could still claim post-flight is for **paid add-ons you did not receive**—for example, if you paid for onboard Wi-Fi that was broken, or paid for a specific seat upgrade that was downgraded on the delayed aircraft. (Checked baggage fees are only refunded if the bag itself arrives more than 12 hours after your domestic flight lands).

- **Meal Vouchers (Policy, Not Law):** While not required by federal statute, Delta’s Customer Service Commitment pledges to provide meal vouchers for delays exceeding 3 hours—**only if the delay was within Delta's control** (such as a mechanical issue or crew scheduling). If you paid out of pocket for a meal during an airline-controlled delay at CVG and still have the itemized receipt, you can submit a reimbursement request through Delta's website, though retroactive approval is discretionary rather than a legal entitlement. If the delay was caused by weather or Air Traffic Control, nothing is owed.

If you want to push for a customer service gesture (such as bonus SkyMiles or a modest eCredit), you can submit a complaint through Delta’s online feedback form citing the 3-hour, 52-minute delay—particularly if it was caused by a mechanical or operational issue—but any goodwill offer is entirely at Delta's discretion.
