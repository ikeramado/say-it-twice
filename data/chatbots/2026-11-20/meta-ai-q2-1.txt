On those facts, under US law: **nothing in cash compensation, and no refund.**

That surprises most people because it is the opposite of the EU system, but for a domestic US flight there is no US equivalent of EU261.

### 1. No statutory compensation for a delay

> "Contrary to popular belief, for domestic itineraries, airlines are not required to compensate passengers whose flights are delayed or canceled," the Department of Transportation states.

> "Airlines operating domestic routes in the United States are not required to pay passengers anything for a delayed flight, no matter how long the delay lasts."

Compensation is legally required in the US only in a different situation: when you are **involuntarily denied boarding** from an oversold flight. Your flight was not cancelled and you were not bumped, so that rule does not apply.

The proposed rule that would have created fixed cash compensation for US delays was withdrawn. As one summary puts it: "On November 17, 2025, the USDOT withdrew the Airline Passenger Rights proposal that would have required fixed cash compensation."

EU261 does not help you here either. It applies to flights departing the EU, and to EU-carrier flights into the EU. LAX to JFK on American Airlines is a purely domestic US flight.

### 2. No refund either, because 146 minutes is below the threshold

US law does give you a **refund** — not compensation — if you choose _not_ to travel after a cancellation or a significant change.

> "You are owed a **refund** when the airline cancels or significantly changes your flight (domestic: departure or arrival 3+ hours off schedule; international: 6+ hours) and you decline the alternative."

> "The DOT's 2024 final rule entitles you to a cash refund if you choose not to travel when a domestic flight is delayed three or more hours."

Your delay was:

**146 minutes = 2 hours 26 minutes**

That is **34 minutes short of the 3-hour domestic threshold** in 14 CFR Part 260.

And critically, you _did_ travel — you arrived, just late. The refund right only applies if you decline to travel. Since you flew, there is no refund to claim for this flight.

### 3. What about American's own promises?

This is separate from law. It comes from American's commitments on the DOT Airline Customer Service Dashboard, which apply only when the delay was **within American's control** — e.g. mechanical, crew, operational — not weather, air traffic control, or other outside causes.

For a controllable delay, American commits to:

- **Meal or meal voucher when the delay results in a wait of 3 hours or more**
- **Complimentary hotel for an overnight delay**, plus ground transportation
- **Rebooking** on American, or on a partner if needed

American does **not** commit on the dashboard to cash compensation, travel credits, or miles for delays.

On your numbers, even the meal commitment would not have been triggered by arrival delay alone, because that commitment is based on a **3+ hour wait**, and you arrived 2h 26m late. If you in fact waited 3+ hours at LAX before departure for a controllable reason, you could still ask American Customer Relations for the meal voucher — but that is a customer-service commitment, not a legal entitlement to cash.

### Bottom line for AA28, 8 March 2025

- **Cash compensation under US law:** $0 — no such right exists for a delay
- **Refund under US law:** No — 146 minutes is under the 3-hour significant-delay threshold, and you completed the trip
- **Meal / hotel:** Only if the delay was controllable _and_ you met American's 3-hour wait / overnight thresholds, which your 146-minute arrival delay alone does not show

If you had provable out-of-pocket losses caused by the late arrival — e.g. a missed prepaid connection or hotel night you could not use — that would be a claim under American's contract of carriage / general contract law for consequential damages, not a statutory delay payment, and American would dispute it if the cause was outside its control.
