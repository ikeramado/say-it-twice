Under U.S. Department of Transportation (DOT) regulations, you are legally entitled to a **full monetary refund** of your ticket and any unused ancillary fees, returned to your original form of payment.

Department of Transportation

Because your flight was canceled and you chose not to accept any alternative transportation, U.S. federal law (including the DOT's Automatic Refund Rule, which took full effect in October 2024) establishes the following rights:

### 1. Full Refund of Ticket and Fees

- **Base Fare & Taxes:** You are owed 100% of the unused ticket purchase price, including all government-imposed taxes and airline-imposed fees—even if you purchased a non-refundable ticket (such as Basic Economy).

    Department of Transportation

- **Ancillary Fees:** You are also entitled to a full refund of any extra fees you paid for services you could not use due to the cancellation, such as checked baggage fees, seat selection fees, or onboard Wi-Fi.

- **Regardless of Cause:** This right applies no matter _why_ Delta canceled the flight (whether due to mechanical issues, staffing, or severe weather).

    US Airways Center


### 2. Cash/Original Payment vs. Vouchers

- **No Forced eCredits:** Delta cannot force you to accept a travel credit or voucher in place of a monetary refund. They may only issue a voucher if you explicitly opt for one.

    US Airways Center

- **Automatic Issuance:** Under DOT rules, if you reject or do not respond to an airline's offer for rebooking or a voucher and do not fly, the airline is required to issue the refund automatically without you needing to jump through hoops. _(Note: If you booked through a third-party travel agency rather than directly with Delta, the merchant of record that processed your payment is responsible for issuing the refund.)_

    US Airways Center


### 3. Legal Refund Deadlines

- **Credit Card Purchases:** Within **7 business days**.

    US Airways Center

- **Other Payment Methods (Cash, Check, Debit):** Within **20 calendar days**.

    US Airways Center


### What You Are _Not_ Entitled To Under U.S. Law

Unlike regulations in the European Union (EU261) or the UK (UK261), **U.S. federal law does not require airlines to pay additional cash compensation** for inconvenience, lost time, or consequential damages (such as missed hotel nights, rental cars, or cruise departures) resulting from a canceled domestic flight.

Travel Tourister

Additionally, because you opted not to travel rather than wait for a rebooked flight, Delta is not legally obligated under federal law to cover meals or hotel accommodations.

### Next Steps if You Haven't Received Your Money

If Delta has not refunded your original payment method—or if they converted your ticket into a Delta eCredit without your consent—you can:

1. Submit a direct refund request through Delta’s official refund portal using your ticket/confirmation number.

    Travel Tourister

2. File a formal consumer complaint with the **U.S. Department of Transportation (DOT)** online, which legally requires the airline to respond to you and the agency within 30 to 60 days.

    Travel Tourister

3. Initiate a credit card chargeback with your card issuer citing services not rendered.
