## **1. Your legal entitlement: a full refund**

Under **14 CFR § 260.6**, when an airline cancels a flight, passengers are entitled to a refund if they do not accept alternative transportation or a travel credit.

For your DL309 flight (Honolulu–Atlanta), this means:

- **100% of the ticket price:** Delta must refund the amount you paid.

- **Original form of payment:** The refund must generally go back to the original payment method.

- **Taxes and fees:** The refund must include applicable taxes and fees associated with the unused ticket.

- **No travel credit requirement:** Delta cannot force you to accept a voucher or credit instead of a refund.


You do not have to prove that Delta was responsible for the circumstances causing the cancellation. The cancellation itself triggers the refund entitlement.

## **2. When should Delta have refunded you?**

The applicable refund deadlines were:

|Original payment method|Refund deadline|
|---|---|
|Credit card|7 business days|

These deadlines apply once the refund becomes due under the regulation. If Delta offered an alternative flight and you did not accept it or travel on it, the refund was still required.

The DOT also requires refunds to be automatic in these circumstances. You should not have needed to submit a conventional refund request first.

## **3. Are you entitled to additional compensation?**

This is an important distinction.

US federal law generally does not require airlines to pay passengers additional cash compensation simply because a flight was cancelled.

Your rights break down as follows:

|Type of compensation|Legal entitlement|
|---|---|
|Full ticket refund|Yes|
|Refund of unused ancillary services|Yes|
|Additional compensation for inconvenience|Generally no|
|Compensation for lost vacation time|Generally no|
|Punitive damages simply because of cancellation|No automatic entitlement|
|Hotel and meal expenses|Not generally guaranteed by federal law|

Delta may have additional obligations under its Contract of Carriage, particularly if the cancellation was within its control. Those obligations are separate from the statutory refund entitlement.
